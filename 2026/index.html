      Wakimoto realization for Lie superalgebras and SUSY analogues
      Representations of Lie algebras can often be constructed using differential operators. A remarkable infinite-dimensional analogue of this idea is the Wakimoto realization, which describes affine Lie algebras in terms of free fields. In this talk, I will begin with a simple example involving the Lie algebra sl2 and explain the basic idea behind Wakimoto realizations. I will then introduce that for basic classical Lie superalgebras and their supersymmetric analogues, and discuss how the classical construction extends to N=1 supersymmetric affine vertex algebras associated with Lie superalgebras.  The talk is intended to be introductory, and no prior knowledge of vertex algebras will be assumed. This talk is based on my preprint (arXiv:2608.22340) and ongoing joint work with Naoki Genra and Shigenori Nakatsuka.

      Deformation quantization as Frechet algebras.
       In this talk, I will describe  a Frechet topology on the space of formal smooth functions on a symplectic manifold X. For any  star product making it a formal deformation quantization of X, I will show that the star product is jointly continuous, and making it a Frechet algebra. I will explain that the canonical trace of any formal deformation quantization is continuous under this Frechet topology. I will also show a quantum Weierstrass theorem on Kähler manifolds which says under each such semi-norm, every formal smooth function can be approximated by quantum polynomials.

      Concrete Functions in Private Simultaneous Quantum Messages
      Private simultaneous messages (PSM) provide a minimal model of noninteractive secure multiparty computation. Its quantum analogue, private simultaneous quantum messages (PSQM), was introduced in Ref. [1], and some progress has been made in the literature. However, their communication complexity of PSQM protocols for concrete functions remains poorly understood. In this talk, I consider the communication complexity of PSQM protocols for several familiar total functions such as equality, majority, multi-bit AND, and demonstrate quantum advantages as well as structural limitations. This talk is based on Ref. [2].

      Takafumi Saikawa (Nagoya Math)
      Currying for representing and formalizing circuit wiring slides
      When can one interpret the outcomes of a quantum measurement as revealing a pre-existing objective property? Using the recently developed formalism of quantum measurement retrodiction, we provide a quantitative treatment of this question: for any POVM and faithful prior state, we construct a positive semidefinite bilinear form that quantifies the non-objectivity of every real-valued outcome feature through the disagreement between its predictive value and its retrodictive counterpart. We show that this form decomposes exactly into the sum of two positive semidefinite bilinear forms: an unsharpness form and an asymmetry form given by Wigner--Yanase skew information. The total form vanishes precisely on those outcome features that can be interpreted, relative to the prior, as revealing pre-existing properties; in particular, it vanishes identically if and only if the POVM is sharp and commutes with the prior. Finally, under maps that preserve the prior and are covariant under its modular group, asymmetry cannot increase, and any loss of asymmetry is offset by at least as much unsharpness, so that total non-objectivity cannot decrease.

      Munan Zhang (SDS, CUHK-SZ)
      Quantum thermodynamics with uncertain equilibrium
      The resource-theoretic approach to quantum thermodynamics typically assumes perfect knowledge of the thermal equilibrium state, an idealization incompatible with finite experimental precision. We develop a framework for equilibrium uncertainty by representing the equilibrium reference as a set of candidate states. Under a generic geometric condition, we prove a no-go theorem that sharply limits athermality ``purification'': universal conversion from an uncertain athermal state into a definite target with an exactly known equilibrium is either trivial or impossible. We then derive exact one-shot entropic characterizations of work extraction and formation for two work-storage models, a clean battery with known equilibrium and a dirty battery with uncertain equilibrium. Both models exhibit strong asymptotic irreversibility even under arbitrarily small uncertainty. An explicit example reveals two distinct extremes: clean batteries display a bound-entanglement-like phenomenon, with positive formation cost but zero extractable work, whereas dirty batteries allow positive work extraction but require infinite formation cost. These phenomena show that equilibrium uncertainty is not a minor perturbation of the standard theory, but a structural ingredient that fundamentally reshapes the limits of quantum thermodynamics.

      Quantum universality with maximal torus setting
      We present a new criterion for the universality of fixed sampled sets of qudit gates containing the standard maximal torus in U(d) or SU(d). The complexity of our certificate method is restricted in polynomial steps. Our results are at both group-theoretic and Lie-algebraic levels.  At the group-theoretic level, we prove that the universality can be certified by checking triviality of the diagonal matrix unital *-algebra. At the Lie-algebraic level, we show that there is a 2 threshold between the identity component and all other components, leading to an effective algorithm for certifying universality. This is joint work with Jing-Song Huang and Yinuo Xue.

      Gen Yue (CUHK)
      Categorical Foundations of Topological Orders: Condensation Completion and Symmetry Enrichment
      This talk explores topological defects, symmetry enrichment, and gauging within a unified categorical framework. We explain how condensation completion reconstructs the full defect theory of a bosonic, anomaly-free topological order in (2+1) dimensions from its particle category, illustrated through explicit calculations and lattice realizations. Using the representation principle, we describe symmetry-enriched topological orders as categorical representations and generalized gauging through Morita equivalence. For finite-group symmetries, we establish an explicit equivalence between the gauged particle category and non-degenerate extension. We also present a criterion for self-dual gauging of (1+1)-dimensional Rep(G) symmetry-protected topological phases. Finally, we interpret anyon condensation as gauging a canonical higher symmetry, linking defect completion to transformations between phases.

      Kohtaro Kato (Nagoya Informatics)
      Information-Theoretic Formulation of Topological Order
      Topologically ordered phases in two-dimensional gapped quantum systems provide a fundamental example of quantum phases that cannot be understood within the conventional framework of symmetry breaking. Their universal properties are widely believed to be described by topological quantum field theories (TQFTs). However, establishing this description directly from the microscopic properties of quantum many-body states remains a challenging problem. In this talk, I will discuss an information-theoretic approach to this problem based on the entanglement bootstrap. The basic idea is to characterize a gapped quantum state through universal constraints on its local reduced density matrices, motivated by the area law of entanglement. Remarkably, these local entropic constraints are already sufficient to derive nontrivial global structures associated with topological order. In particular, one can identify superselection sectors and derive the fusion rules of anyonic excitations without assuming an underlying TQFT or microscopic quasiparticle description.
      I will explain the basic framework and illustrate how topological data emerge from purely quantum-information-theoretic properties of the ground state. I will also discuss what is currently known about the scope and limitations of this approach, including the issue of spurious topological entanglement entropy. Finally, I will briefly discuss ongoing work toward connecting the entanglement-bootstrap framework with operator-algebraic approaches to infinite quantum systems, with the aim of developing a unified formulation of topological order in the thermodynamic limit.

      Yiwei Xu (SDS, CUHK-SZ)
      Finite attainment and efficient computation of regularized quantum channel relative entropy
      Quantum channel relative entropy can be nonadditive, making its regularization essential for asymptotic analysis. We study when the regularized value is attained at finite blocklength and how efficiently it can be approximated. For the first question, we establish a sharp dichotomy: the regularized value is either attained already at the single-copy level, implying additivity at every blocklength, or is never attained at any finite blocklength. Notably, a simple two-copy additivity test distinguishes between these alternatives for qubit channels and for channel pairs in arbitrary dimensions admitting an interior optimizer. We further show that both the interiority condition and finite nonattainment are generic, holding for almost every channel pair. For the second question, we establish explicit convergence rates for approximating the regularized channel relative entropy. For fixed channel dimensions, O˜(ε−3/2) channel copies suffice to approximate the regularized value to additive accuracy ε. Alternatively, a semidefinite hierarchy achieves the same accuracy at level O˜(ε−2), yielding a certified polynomial-time approximation. Together, these results reveal the intriguing structure and generic behavior of regularized channel relative entropy, sharpening our understanding of both its analytical and computational aspects.

      Zhiwei Song (SDS, CUHK-SZ)
      Approximate majorization and high-order capacity of quantum depolarizing channels
      A central challenge in quantum information theory is to determine whether entanglement provides an advantage for information processing when coherent operations across multiple channel uses are allowed. For depolarizing channels, King's proof of the additivity of the minimum output entropy rules out such an advantage for classical communication at leading asymptotic order, while leaving open the possibility of an advantage for higher orders. In this work, we conjecture a majorization relation for the outputs of depolarizing channels and prove an approximate version of this conjecture. Our proof combines an exact ordering of shifted log-determinants with an anti-concentration bound for the spectrum of the product-state output, refining King's analysis based on Schatten norms. An additional logarithmic second-moment estimate controls arbitrary channel outputs. As a direct application, we obtain a tight third-order expansion for the classical capacity of quantum depolarizing channels. Consequently, entangled codewords and collective decoding can improve the logarithm of the message size over the product-coding strategy by at most O(1) bits, sharpening our understanding of the role of entanglement in the finite-blocklength regime.

      Jianchao Zhang (SDS, CUHK-SZ)
      Schur–Weyl Reduction of Finite-Copy Precision Bounds for Multiparameter Quantum Estimation
      In multiparameter quantum-state estimation, measurements optimized for different parameters may be incompatible. We study finite-copy precision bounds for measurements performed jointly on fixed blocks of m identical copies drawn from a much larger sample. The relevant finite-block quantities are the locally unbiased tight Cramér–Rao bound and its Nagaoka–Hayashi (NH) semidefinite relaxation, compared across block sizes after multiplication by m. Direct evaluation is difficult because the dimension of the m-copy Hilbert space grows exponentially with m. Using Schur–Weyl duality, we remove permutation multiplicity spaces while retaining their dimensions as weights. Compression and recovery quantum channels establish the statistical equivalence of the full and reduced models under arbitrary collective measurements. Consequently, both finite-block bounds retain their exact values in the reduced formulations, whose physical operator representation grows polynomially for fixed single-copy dimension. For the tight bound, we also correct finite-ray dual solutions using an exact quadratic identity to obtain rigorous upper and lower bounds. At each fixed block size and parameter dimension, sufficiently fine meshes are primal-feasible and near-optimal sampled dual solutions have uniformly bounded search radii, yielding a certified O(δ2) enclosure width in the projective covering radius δ. Qubit examples demonstrate the computational reach of the reduced NH formulation and the quadratic scaling of the tight bound correction.

      Spectral Sequences Meet Topological Phases: Domain Wall Decorations and Interacting Topological Superconductors
      The Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence (AHSS) provides a unifying mathematical framework for classifying symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases, with a transparent physical interpretation: the decorated domain wall construction. In this talk, I will first show how the E2 page of the spectral sequence encodes decorations by lower-dimensional SPT states at domain wall junctions, reducing to the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for bosonic group-cohomology SPTs. Explicit cochain-level formulas for the differentials yield physical consequences such as Lieb–Schultz–Mattis theorems and classifications of symmetry-enriched gauge theories. I will then discuss the generalization to fermionic systems, where four layers of decoration data (n1, n2, n3, ν4​) lead to a systematic classification of three-dimensional interacting topological crystalline superconductors, including those protected by all 230 space groups.

      An elliptic deformation of rationals and continued fractions
      We construct an elliptic deformation Q a,b; q,p (x) of each positive rational number x by attaching Schlosser's small elliptic weights w a,b; q,p ( s,t) to prefixes of the Stern--Brocot word associated to x. We also construct regular and negative elliptic continued fractions. The degeneration limit p,a,b →0 recovers the q>1-deformed rationals and continued fractions introduced by Morier-Genoud and Ovsienko (Forum Math. Sigma, 2020). For rationals x, the numerator and the denominator of the elliptic rationals are the closure polynomials, or quiver F-polynomials, of the associated oriented type-A quivers. This talk is based on arXiv:2611.10104.

      Baichu Yu (Quantum Science Center of Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area)
      Total Information of Two Measurements via Partial Information Decomposition and Its Relation to the Holevo Bound
      Partial information decomposition (PID) provides a framework for understanding how information about a common source is distributed among multiple variables, typically in terms of redundant, unique, and synergistic information. A central challenge in this framework is the characterization of total information when the joint probability distribution of the variables is unavailable. This issue is particularly relevant in quantum information theory, where the outcomes of incompatible measurements do not, in general, admit a joint probability distribution. In this work, we employ the Harder–Salge–Polani (HSP) projected-redundancy construction to circumvent this difficulty and define a total-information quantity for two measurements using only their respective joint distributions with the source. We then apply this construction to finite-dimensional classical-quantum ensembles. We prove that the defined total information is no smaller than the accessible information and no greater than the Holevo quantity. The upper bound is derived through a recovery-theoretic inequality that connects a projection information to the information loss induced by a quantum measurement, using Stinespring dilation and measured-relative-entropy recovery. Our result shows that the HSP construction can characterize more of the information in a quantum ensemble than is captured by any single measurement, while remaining bounded by the total quantum mutual information.

      Ranyiliu Chen (Quantum Science Center of Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area)
      A non-robust quantum correlation self-test
      We show that exact self-testing of a quantum correlation does not imply robust self-testing. Mančinska and Schmidt previously established such a separation for non-local games, but their obstruction to robustness arises from two distinct optimal correlations, with the self-tested one remaining robust. We establish the separation for a single correlation. Our proof is operator-algebraic. The main technical contribution is a general construction that associates a synchronous binary correlation to any finitely presented C*-algebra with unitary generators and real-coefficient relations. The implementing states of the correlation correspond exactly to the tracial states on the algebra. We apply this construction to an algebra with a unique finite-dimensional tracial state and a distinct amenable tracial state. By a characterization established by Zhao and independently by Kar, the resulting correlation is a self-test but not a robust self-test.

      Duo Xu (Nagoya Informatics)
      Decryption Keys in Public-Key Encryption
      We propose the first error-tolerant secure key leasing (SKL) scheme for public-key encryption. As in prior SKL protocols, the scheme involves a lessor and a lessee. The lessor encodes its secret key into quantum states and leases the key to the lessee, while retaining the ability to request its return at a later time and verify whether the return is honest. Our protocol remains functional even when the leased quantum secret key is subject to noise. In particular, the lessee can correctly decrypt ciphertexts, and the lessor can correctly verify the return of the secret key whenever the amount of error is below a certain threshold. Moreover, our construction does not modify the original encoding of the secret key and therefore introduces no additional overhead in quantum information processing. Our main contribution is a framework for analyzing the trade-off between robustness against errors and security. We establish a connection between the security of error-tolerant SKL and that of error-tolerant certified deletion with shortened codes, a relatively less explored concept in coding theory.

      Hesam Hosseini (IE CUHK)
      Conditional Independence of a Beam Splitter Output with Quantum Memory
      The classical Kac–Bernstein theorem provides a remarkable characterization of Gaussian distributions: if two independent random variables have independent sum and difference, then they must be Gaussian. A natural question is whether a similar characterization holds in quantum systems, particularly when quantum side information is available. In this talk, I will discuss a quantum extension of this principle for bosonic systems with quantum memories. We consider two independent inputs mixed through a beam splitter and ask what can be inferred about the input states when the outputs are conditionally independent given the memories. I will explain how this condition reveals a common Gaussian structure underlying the inputs. We will also see how and when entanglement between the main system and the memory can exist.

      Masahito Hayashi (SDS, CUHK-SZ/IQA/Nagoya Math)
      Readability of mathematics manuscripts and the ReadabilityKit revision method
      In mathematics manuscripts written wholly or partly by AI, readers may be unable to tell what a term refers to, which object a condition applies to, or how statements they understand individually fit together. We propose a method that records where readers get stuck and what they cannot determine, then checks the revised text and its mathematical grounds to see whether each difficulty has been resolved. We formulate two working hypotheses about how barriers to understanding arise and how an audit can miss them. First, AI may handle several conditions and relationships together during mathematical reasoning, and this grouping may appear in the manuscript as compressed expressions that convey several pieces of information. Second, an AI auditor may fill in an explanation from context and overlook that the manuscript leaves the explanation unstated. These hypotheses inform the design of ten perspectives for characterizing barriers to understanding. We use this framework in a readability revision method with three stages, Audit, Plan, and Revise. Audit locates barriers and records what readers cannot determine, together with the evidence for each finding. Plan characterizes each issue using the relevant perspectives and decides what to change, where, and how. Revise uses the plan and related examples to revise the manuscript, then checks whether readers can follow the necessary explanations and whether the original mathematical assumptions and claims are preserved. To enable AI to carry out the method, we assembled a kit containing the instructions for each stage, explanations of the ten perspectives, and examples to guide revision. We call this kit ReadabilityKit.
